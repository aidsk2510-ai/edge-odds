Where the numbers come from

Every NBA box score this season and last season (from the free sportsdataverse data set, which copies ESPN box scores). Injuries come from ESPN's injury report. It all updates every few hours, and none of it uses odds credits.

The four stat views

This season and Last 14 days are exactly what happened. Last season is last year's per-game numbers. Projected is the one we recommend: this season's numbers, with 12 games of last season mixed in. Early in the year that keeps a hot or cold week from fooling you; by midseason it's almost all this year. Rookies use only this season.

9-Cat rankings

For each category (FG%, FT%, 3PM, PTS, REB, AST, STL, BLK, TO), we compare a player to the average of the top players who'd be rostered in your league (teams × roster spots). The difference is shown as a z-score: +1.0 means one "step" better than a typical rostered player, -1.0 one step worse. Turnovers are flipped, so fewer is better. FG% and FT% count by impact: a 60% shooter on 15 shots a game helps your team's FG% far more than a 60% shooter on 3 shots. A player's Value is his z-scores added up. If you punt a category, it's left out of Value.

Points rankings

FPPG is each player's per-game stats times your league's scoring. ESPN and Yahoo defaults are built in, or tap Edit scoring to match your league. ROS FP is FPPG × the games we expect him to play the rest of the season.

Player metrics

USG% (usage rate): the share of his team's plays he finishes with a shot, free throws or a turnover while he's on the floor; 20% is average, 30%+ is a team's go-to scorer. TS% (true shooting): scoring efficiency, counting 3s and free throws; around 58% is average. eFG%: field goal % with 3s worth 1.5 makes. Role: starter, key bench (22+ minutes), rotation (14+) or fringe. Team minutes rank shows where he sits in his team's rotation.

How the Trade Machine decides

1. Each player's value per game (9-cat Value or FPPG) is compared to a waiver-level player: the average player just outside the rostered pool, the one you could pick up for free.
2. That's multiplied by the games he's expected to play the rest of the season: his team's games left, minus games he'll miss if he's injured (from his return date), times how often he's played this season and last (durability).
3. Add up each side. More players doesn't automatically win: if you get 2 players for 1, you have to cut someone (a waiver-level player), and if you get 1 for 2 you get a free spot to fill from waivers. Comparing to waiver level handles both.
4. For 9-cat we also show how each category changes per game, so you can tell if a trade fits your punt build.
5. We flag the things numbers can miss: small samples, minutes or usage going up or down, injured teammates opening up shots, and players listed behind him at his position.
Edge's take sends this exact breakdown to our AI to explain it in plain English. It can only use these numbers; it doesn't read news.

What it can't know

Your league's waiver wire, your opponent's needs, trades and coaching changes before they show up in box scores, and playoff schedules. Use it as a strong second opinion, not a guarantee.
